# Distribution — getting it in front of people

Reach for this when **check 10** (distribution sovereignty and answer-engine
presence) came back RISKY or FAIL, or when the idea is sound and nobody has
found it.

Building stopped being the constraint some time ago. Reaching people did not.

**Before you change channel, check the message.** If a channel has been run at
volume and produced silence, run the no-response diagnostic in
[`index.md`](index.md) first. Switching channels is the expensive way to
discover that the claim was invisible in all of them. For an institutional
buyer — a venue, a clinic, a council — the channel is a person, and the craft is
in [`selling.md`](selling.md).

---

## 1. One fit is not enough — Balfour's four fits

Brian Balfour's framework, and the most useful correction to "we have
product-market fit, now we scale":

1. **Market–product** — the product serves a market that wants it
2. **Product–channel** — the product is *shaped* to travel through its channel
3. **Channel–model** — the channel's cost is compatible with the price
4. **Model–market** — the price is compatible with what that market pays

Each constrains the others, and companies stall because they have one or two
rather than all four. This is a practitioner framework, not research, and it
earns its place by being the thing that explains most stalls.

**The rule founders break:** you do not pick a channel for a product. The
product and channel co-evolve, so a channel decision is a product decision.

**The channel–model arithmetic** decides more than anything else here. A
$30/month product cannot support a sales conversation. A $50,000 contract will
not close through content alone. Compute what a customer can cost you, from
price and payback target, before choosing how to reach them — this is why
`pricing.md` comes first in the sequence.

## 2. Prefer loops to funnels

A funnel is a thing you pour into. A loop reinvests its output as its next
input: a user produces something (content, an invite, data, a public artifact)
that brings the next user.

For each candidate loop, write down the input, the step, the output, and
crucially **how the output becomes the next input**. If it does not close, it is
a funnel with a nice diagram, and its cost per acquisition will rise over time
rather than fall.

## 3. Know which part of the curve you are selling to

Rogers' *Diffusion of Innovations* (1962) and Bass's formal model (1969, one of
the ten most-cited papers in *Management Science*'s first fifty years) both say
adoption is driven by two populations: **innovators**, who act on the product's
own merits, and **imitators**, who act on other people's adoption.

Two operational consequences:

- **Early traction is not predictive.** Innovators tolerate rough edges and buy
  on novelty. Their enthusiasm is exactly the signal that produces Eisenmann's
  *False Positive* failure (`frameworks.md §13`).
- **Imitators need proof, not features.** Case studies, named logos, peer
  recommendation and a category they already understand. The messaging that won
  the first hundred customers will actively repel the next thousand.

Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* is the practitioner treatment of that discontinuity:
win a narrow beachhead segment completely rather than a thin slice of everyone.
For a bootstrap-path business, the beachhead may be the whole business, and
that is a legitimate destination rather than a stalled start.

## 4. Choose few channels and go deep

A working go-to-market has three or four channels that work, not twelve that
almost do. Pick on two criteria: **can this team actually run it**, and **does
its cost fit the price** (channel–model, again).

For a bootstrap path the channel must be runnable by one person with no budget,
which in practice means founder-led: an owned audience, communities the founder
is genuinely part of, partnerships, and presence where the answer engines look.

## 5. Be present where software is now discovered

Buyers increasingly shortlist by asking an assistant. Those systems synthesize
from Reddit, Hacker News, developer forums, comparison content and review sites,
so absence from those surfaces is absence from the shortlist — see
`market-data.md §9`.

Two distinct pieces of work: **be discussed** in the places that get
synthesized, and **be machine-readable** where it counts (clear comparison
pages, explicit pricing, structured data).

Assistant app directories are a real channel and a real dependency. Treat them
as upside. Never as the plan — the platform is also your most likely competitor,
which is check 10's whole point.

## What "fixed" looks like

One channel that demonstrably carries customers at a cost the price supports,
with a loop rather than a funnel behind it, and a plan for the transition from
innovators to imitators that does not assume the same message works for both.
