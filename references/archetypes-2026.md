# Archetypes & Precedents — 2026

Two jobs for this file. First, so the brief can say *"your shape is wrong, and
here is the shape that is working"* rather than only cataloguing risk. Second,
so precedent cases are drawn from a verified list instead of being invented.

**All figures verified 2026-08-22.** Re-verify anything load-bearing before
quoting; company facts move fast. Never cite a precedent from this file without
a web search confirming its current state.

---

## Part 1 — Winning shapes

Every "What You'd Do Instead / Strategic Options" section must map the subject
onto the nearest archetype below, or explicitly state that it matches none.

### A. Vertical AI owning a regulated workflow
Narrow industry + regulatory or professional gatekeeping + becomes the system of
record + sold through people who already have the relationships.

- **Why it works:** the labs cannot ship into it, the data compounds, and the
  buyer is buying liability absorption as much as capability.
- **Evidence:** Harvey — 82 staff and a $715M valuation in 2023 → 350 staff,
  ~$190M ARR, $11B valuation by March 2026. Harvey, Legora and Sierra each
  crossed $100M ARR within nine months of one another.
- **Checks it passes:** 1, 5, 6, 9, 11, 12.
- **Recommend when:** the founder has domain access others cannot buy.

### B. Outcome-priced agent replacing a cost line
Sold as a resolved unit of work, priced per outcome, replacing a headcount line
rather than sitting beside it.

- **Evidence:** Intercom's Fin reached $100M ARR at $0.99 per resolution;
  HubSpot moved its Customer Agent to $0.50 per resolved conversation in April
  2026; Sierra crossed $150M ARR; Decagon reported $50M+ ARR with a $250M
  Series D in January 2026.
- **Requires:** a measurable outcome, an owner of the cost line, and a real
  accuracy bar (check 7).
- **Recommend when:** the buyer already has a budget for humans doing this.

### C. Service-as-software / AI-enabled rollup
Buy the services business, re-margin it with AI, repeat.

- **Evidence:** >$3B deployed to the strategy; General Catalyst's Creation Fund
  went from $800M to $1.5B, backing rollups in accounting, legal, IT services
  and property management; ~half of the 30 largest US accounting firms carry PE
  or alternative-practice money by early 2026; Crete acquired 10+ accounting
  firms in 2025; Long Lake's $6.3B take-private of Amex GBT in May 2026 is the
  largest deal to date. Re-margined businesses have drawn 12–18× exit multiples.
- **Recommend when:** the buyer wants the outcome and will not adopt a tool; the
  industry is fragmented; the founder can operate, not just build.
- **This is the most under-recommended pivot in 2026.** When a founder is trying
  to sell software into an industry that has never bought software, delivering
  the work and automating it behind the scenes is very often the better shape.

### D. Developer-velocity products
The fastest-growing category by revenue, repeatedly.

- **Evidence:** Cursor hit $100M ARR in 12 months, $500M by June 2025, $1B by
  November 2025, ~$2B by February 2026 — the fastest B2B SaaS company to $1B ARR
  on record. Lovable went $0→$500M ARR in ~14 months. Bolt reached $100M in 14
  months; Replit's agent went $10M→$100M in six.
- **Caution:** also the most contested category, with the labs shipping directly
  into it. Speed of iteration is the moat, which means it is a moat you rent.

### E. Bootstrap niche monopoly
One person, one narrow audience, priced properly.

- **Evidence:** median profitable micro-SaaS ~$4.2k MRR at 64–76% margins;
  median 12–18 months to $10k MRR. The niches stay open precisely because they
  are too small to interest a funded team.
- **Recommend when:** the founder is the customer, the audience is reachable
  without paid acquisition, and the goal is ownership rather than scale.

## Part 2 — Losing shapes

- **Thin wrapper + horizontal SMB + $20–50/mo + per-seat pricing.** The densest
  failure cluster in the current cohort. Checks 1, 2, 4 and 6 typically all FAIL.
- **"We'll be the layer that…"** Bridge products between today's model
  limitations and tomorrow's. The limitation is the business, and it has an
  expiry date set by someone else.
- **Consumer AI subscription with no habit loop.** Priced against a 21.1% annual
  retention prior whether the founder knows it or not.
- **Agent marketplace / directory with no supply lock-in.** Two-sided
  chicken-and-egg on top of AI-era commoditization.
- **Enterprise AI pilot-ware.** Sells pilots, never crosses to production. The
  95% number lives here.
- **Model-as-product without capital.** Competing on base capability against
  entities that raised tens of billions.

## Part 3 — Precedent library

Use two or three per brief, chosen for *structural* similarity to the subject —
not surface similarity. Always confirm the current state by search before
citing; several of these changed materially inside the last year.

### Deaths and near-deaths

| Company | What happened | The lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Builder.ai** | Microsoft-backed, ~$1.2–1.5B valuation, insolvent May 2025. The "AI" was substantially ~700 engineers in India; revenue was overstated. | Fabricated capability and fabricated revenue are the same failure. Also the strongest argument for this skill's zero-trust step. |
| **Humane** | AI Pin shipped April 2024 to poor reviews; assets sold to HP February 2025 (reported $116M, some sources $166M — verify) after raising $230M+. | Hardware abstracting a capability the phone already has, beaten by the phone. Nine months from launch to sale. |
| **Jasper** | Horizontal AI writing; commoditized when ChatGPT shipped the category natively. | Still the cleanest "category absorbed by the lab" case, but it is 2023–24 vintage. Do not use it as the *only* precedent. |
| **Character.AI** | Survived commercially, then was regulated into a different product: open-ended chat removed for under-18s by 2025-11-25, platform-wide moderation sweep February 2026. | **A regulatory precedent, not a consolidation one.** Product-market fit does not protect you from a compliance obligation. Use whenever check 11 is in play. |
| **Stability AI** | Structural margin compression as open weights proliferated. | Being upstream of a commoditizing input is not a position. |
| **Windsurf** | 2025: an OpenAI acquisition collapsed, Google took the founders in a ~$2.4B licence-and-hire, Cognition bought what remained. | The modern AI exit is frequently not an exit for the company. |
| **Reverse acquihires generally** | Google, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta spent $20B+ between March 2024 and January 2026 hiring founding teams and licensing technology without acquiring the companies. | Founders exit; investors and the entity often do not. If the plan's exit is "acquisition," ask which kind. |

### Compounding businesses

| Company | Position | The lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Harvey** | ~$190M ARR, $11B valuation, 350 staff (March 2026) | Vertical + regulated + workflow ownership + relationship distribution. |
| **Cursor (Anysphere)** | ~$2B ARR February 2026; fastest B2B SaaS to $1B | A "wrapper" that became defensible through usage data and switching cost. Proof that check 1 is about complements, not architecture. |
| **Lovable** | $0→$500M ARR in ~14 months | Distribution and velocity as the moat. Fastest to $400M on record. |
| **Sierra / Decagon** | $150M and $50M+ ARR respectively | Outcome pricing against a cost line the buyer already funds. |
| **Pearl (dental)** | Narrow vertical, regulatory clearance, proprietary imaging data | The durable small-market shape. |

### Base rates to quote alongside

~40% of the recent AI cohort dead inside 24 months; ~70% of micro-SaaS never
clears $1k MRR; 80–95% of thin wrappers failed. Sources and dates in
`market-data.md §1`.

## Using precedents well

- **Structural, not superficial.** "Also an AI company" is not a precedent.
  "Also sold a horizontal tool into SMBs at $30/mo against a native feature" is.
- **Name the year and the number.** A precedent without an outcome is an anecdote.
- **One counter-precedent is worth three confirming ones.** If a company
  succeeded with the shape being criticized, name it and explain what they had
  that the subject does not. That is the difference between analysis and
  pattern-matching.
- **Never invent one.** If a search turns up nothing structurally similar, say
  so — "no close precedent found" is itself a finding, and usually a warning.
